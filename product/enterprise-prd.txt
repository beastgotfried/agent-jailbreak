# Veyra

Enterprise Product Requirements Document
Version 1.0 · 16 October 2026 · Proposed enterprise specification

Observe change. Verify impact. Control response.

## 01. Purpose and document control

### What this document does

This PRD defines the users, outcomes, product scope, requirements, and acceptance tests for an enterprise safety-observability product. It sets the product contract for engineering, design, security, operations, and model-risk teams. Architecture choices support those requirements. They do not replace detailed engineering designs.

### Document contract

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Product | Veyra; short name: Veyra. |
| Scope | Full enterprise general availability, called GA in this document. |
| Status | Proposed specification. No production-readiness approval exists. |
| Accountable functions | Product owner; platform lead; ML lead; security lead; privacy lead; SRE lead. Individual approvers remain unassigned. |
| Release authority | Product, engineering, security, privacy, and SRE approve all GA gates. Model Risk approves any performance claim or policy relaxation. |
| Change control | Each approved change records its reason, affected requirement IDs, owner, and acceptance-test impact. |

| Evidence labels | Verified environment; research evidence; product requirement; unverified assumption. These labels remain separate. |

### Read this before implementation

The current server is a development environment. No product application or product-trained model exists. This PRD does not claim that enterprise scale, hardware attestation, statistical guarantees, customer demand, or certifications are already proven. This document supersedes earlier concept pitches and design notes wherever their claims conflict.

### Approval rule

An unmeasured acceptance test has status **Not demonstrated**, not Pass. A required control cannot be removed to meet a release date. Release exceptions require a named risk owner, expiry date, and compensating control. Tenant isolation and unauthorized-action defects cannot receive an exception.

## 02. Executive product brief

### The customer problem

An enterprise uses safety classifiers to screen AI requests and responses. New traffic, changed models, new policies, or attacks can alter their behavior. Existing scores do not establish whether the classifiers still meet the enterprise's safety objectives. Ground-truth labels often arrive late or cover only a sample.

### The product promise

Veyra observes deployed safety controls, finds behavior changes, gathers evidence, and coordinates an approved response. It distinguishes a statistical signal from confirmed performance loss. It provides operators with a decision record instead of an unexplained risk score.

### The complete enterprise loop

- Receive versioned score events from existing guardrails and gateways.
- Compare current behavior with approved reference periods and independent signals.
- Separate data faults, distribution change, confirmed errors, and missing evidence.
- Open an incident with the affected deployment, evidence, and accountable owner.
- Evaluate a proposed response with labelled data and a locked acceptance policy.
- Apply an authorized change or rollback through a constrained connector.
- Verify the result and preserve an exportable audit record.

### Three operating modes

**Observe:** collect evidence without control over application traffic.

**Advise:** create incidents and propose actions. A person approves changes.

**Enforce:** execute only pre-approved, bounded actions through a customer-controlled enforcement point.

Observe is the default. A passive observer cannot prevent an unsafe response that has already left the application. Inline prevention requires a separate, explicitly integrated enforcement path.

### Core boundaries

The product does not guarantee safe AI, detect every attack, or repair model weights from unlabelled scores. It does not replace the customer's guardrail, incident team, or legal judgement.

## 03. Customers, users, and outcomes

### Primary customer profile

Target organizations already operate AI applications, own a guardrail policy, and maintain an incident process. They need private deployment, accountable changes, and evidence across multiple models or gateways. Buyers include AI platform leaders, security leaders, and model-risk leaders. These are proposed customer profiles, not verified sales demand.

| User | Decision to support | Required outcome |
|---|---|---|
| LinuxONE platform operator | Is the safety-observability service reliable and within capacity? | Native s390x operation, private data paths, and clear service health. |
| AI platform engineer | Does a model or gateway change alter the safety control? | Version-specific comparisons and a controlled rollback path. |
| Trust and safety analyst | Does an alert show a data fault, behavior change, or confirmed error? | Evidence, label coverage, affected scope, and one incident owner. |
| Model-risk reviewer | Does a candidate change preserve the approved risk limits? | A reproducible evaluation with uncertainty and an approval record. |
| SRE on call | Is the monitor itself unavailable or behind? | A separate service alarm and a tested recovery procedure. |
| Auditor or compliance reviewer | Who approved a change, and what evidence supported it? | Read-only, verifiable exports with retained provenance. |

### Proposed product outcomes

- A customer can instrument an approved deployment within one business day after identity and network access exist.
- Every incident identifies the deployment version, policy version, evidence coverage, and next responsible person.
- Every production change has an authorization, test result, execution result, and rollback reference.
- Every tenant can identify missing telemetry instead of mistaking silence for safety.
- Content remains inside the approved data boundary unless the customer explicitly permits an export.

### Product success measures

The north-star metric is the share of active deployments with fresh telemetry, an approved reference, qualified evidence, and a named owner. Secondary metrics include review delay, verified rollback time, and false pages per monitor-hour. Report coverage separately from incident counts.

### Discovery gate

Product validates the problem with at least ten structured interviews and three design partners before commercial GA. Interviews cover current tools, incident frequency, approval delays, false-alert tolerance, and willingness to pay. Discovery results can change positioning; they cannot erase safety controls.

## 04. Positioning, scope, and exclusions

### Positioning

Veyra is a safety-control observability and response platform. Proposed differentiation combines evidence-linked behavior assessment, delayed-label evaluation, governed action, and native LinuxONE deployment. Customer and competitive validation remain required.

### Prior art and competition

Research already describes canary disagreement, sequential change detection, conformal adaptation, and guardrail attestation. Open-source implementations exist. Guardrail vendors, AI gateways, ML observability tools, and governance platforms are adjacent competitors. No claim of an empty market, first invention, or unique algorithm is permitted without a separate competitive study.

### Enterprise GA includes

- Tenant, organization, workspace, deployment, and policy management.
- Score and label intake, reference periods, detectors, and service-health checks.
- Incidents, approvals, evaluation, controlled actions, rollback, and evidence export.
- Enterprise identity, scoped service credentials, privacy controls, and retention.
- Multi-node deployment, capacity controls, disaster recovery, and support procedures.
- Customer-hosted and dedicated private deployments with a qualified LinuxONE data plane.

### Explicit exclusions

- Universal jailbreak prevention or proof that an AI system is safe.
- Unsupervised automatic policy relaxation or self-modification of guardrail weights.
- Employee surveillance, identity inference, or general-purpose content collection.
- Automatic execution of instructions found in prompts, reports, or retrieved documents.
- A model marketplace, malicious-pickle execution service, or general red-team platform.
- A legal-compliance certificate or a claim that a hash chain proves hardware execution.

### Scope discipline

Text safety is the supported decision surface for first enterprise GA. Multimodal and autonomous-agent controls require separate validation and approvals. Connectors can record unsupported modalities, but the console must display **Unsupported coverage**, not imply protection.

## 05. End-to-end user journeys

### Journey A · Connect and observe

1. A tenant administrator creates a workspace and selects its region and retention policy.
2. A platform engineer registers a guardrail, model version, task, score definition, and owner.
3. The engineer sends telemetry through a scoped collector credential.
4. The console validates schema coverage, event counts, and version consistency.
5. A reviewer approves a reference period after it passes data and label checks.
6. Observe mode starts. No application control changes.

### Journey B · Investigate a signal

1. A detector reports a change after its configured evidence window is ready.
2. The incident shows current and reference behavior, uncertainty, missing signals, and affected traffic slices.
3. An analyst checks data faults, deployment changes, repeated sessions, and delayed labels.
4. The analyst classifies the finding and requests labels if evidence remains incomplete.
5. The owner resolves a data fault or proposes a policy or model response.

### Journey C · Approve and verify a response

1. An engineer submits an immutable candidate with a rollback version.
2. A replay job evaluates it against a locked, task-matched holdout.
3. Model Risk reviews performance, subgroup results, confidence intervals, and costs.
4. An authorized approver permits a bounded rollout.
5. The enforcer applies the change with an idempotency key and an expiry.
6. Independent checks verify the outcome. A failed check triggers the approved rollback.

### Journey D · Audit and recover

An auditor exports the evidence package without write access. An SRE rehearses recovery from a recorded checkpoint. Both journeys preserve tenant scope, source versions, action history, and gaps in observation.

## 06. States and decision authority

### Monitor states

| State | Meaning | Permitted response |
|---|---|---|
| Initializing | The stream or reference period is not ready. | Collect evidence. No health assertion. |
| Nominal | Available signals remain within the approved reference policy. | Continue observation. This is not proof of safety. |
| Change suspected | A qualified statistical signal changes. | Open or update an incident; request review. |
| Performance concern | Labelled results breach a configured risk limit. | Propose containment or an approved version change. |
| Insufficient data | Counts, label coverage, or slice coverage do not support a conclusion. | Request evidence; display the gap. |
| Degraded | Telemetry, a detector, a dependency, or the monitor fails. | Raise a service alarm; apply the contracted fallback. |
| Paused | An authorized operator pauses an assessment. | Record reason, scope, expiry, and owner. |

### Incident states

An incident follows **Open → Acknowledged → Investigating → Contained → Resolved**. New evidence can reopen it. A state change requires an actor, timestamp, reason, and related evidence. Resolution does not delete the event history.

### Authority boundaries

- A statistical signal can create an incident. It cannot establish that a request is harmful.
- A score change cannot authorize a more permissive safety threshold.
- An operator can contain within a pre-approved policy. A reviewer must approve a relaxed policy or a new model.
- Low evidence cannot produce a green health indicator.
- Missing telemetry cannot reset the incident state to Nominal.

### Fallback policy

Each deployment declares fail-open, fail-closed, or human-review behavior before Enforce mode starts. The customer risk owner approves it. A service outage cannot silently choose a different fallback.

## 07. Functional requirements · intake

| ID | Product requirement | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| INT-01 | Register each source with tenant, workspace, deployment, model version, task, score contract, and owner. | Intake rejects events for an unknown source. A registered source appears with its exact version. |
| INT-02 | Accept authenticated HTTPS JSON batches and a documented stream adapter. Acknowledge only after durable receipt. | A broker fault returns a retryable failure. No success response precedes durable receipt. |
| INT-03 | Validate types, score ranges, finite numbers, timestamps, payload limits, and schema versions. | Invalid data receives a specific error. NaN, infinity, and unsupported schema versions cannot enter detectors. |
| INT-04 | Support at-least-once delivery with event IDs and deduplication. Do not claim exactly-once processing. | Repeated IDs do not duplicate counts or actions within the 72-hour retry window. Conflicting content receives HTTP 409. |
| INT-05 | Preserve source event time and intake time. Mark late, missing, reordered, and clock-skewed events. | A late-data test retains the record and excludes it from the wrong live window. |
| INT-06 | Receive delayed labels and adjudications with task, provenance, reviewer, confidence, and label version. | Labels attach to the correct event and version. Contradictions enter adjudication rather than overwrite history. |
| INT-07 | Apply per-tenant event, feature, storage, and query quotas. Use explicit backpressure and retries. | One tenant exceeds its limit without exhausting another tenant's allocation. |
| INT-08 | Expose completeness, queue delay, schema errors, sequence gaps, and drop counts. | A stopped collector produces a service-health alert, not a healthy classifier result. |

### Operational rules

The default event contains metadata and scores, not prompt or response text. Optional content requires a tenant policy, approved purpose, retention setting, and data classification. Replay sources carry an immutable replay flag and cannot trigger production actions.

### Interpretation rule

Unlabelled change is evidence of changed behavior only. Labelled evaluation and human review establish whether the change breaches a customer objective.

## 09. Functional requirements · response

| ID | Product requirement | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| RES-01 | Create a deduplicated incident with severity, affected deployment, evidence, owner, and next action. | Related alerts join one incident without hiding distinct model or tenant versions. |
| RES-02 | Support acknowledgement, assignment, escalation, comments, resolution, and reopening. | Every transition appears in an append-only history with a responsible actor. |
| RES-03 | Permit only an allowlisted action set: observe, request review, increase approved sampling, route to review, freeze rollout, or rollback. | Arbitrary commands and prompt-supplied actions fail validation. |
| RES-04 | Require explicit approval for a threshold relaxation, new guardrail, or changed safety policy. | The requester cannot approve the same high-risk change. The complete proposal is bound to the approval. |
| RES-05 | Use idempotency, version preconditions, bounded scope, and expiry for every external action. | Retried actions apply once. Stale policy versions and expired approvals are rejected. |
| RES-06 | Verify the applied state through an independent read path and a post-change observation window. | An unverified action remains Pending or Failed. It cannot appear as completed containment. |
| RES-07 | Roll back to an approved known-good configuration when the declared checks fail. | A rehearsal restores the version and preserves evidence of both the failed change and rollback. |
| RES-08 | Provide a customer-controlled stop switch, break-glass workflow, and maintenance mode. | The stop switch halts new automated actions without deleting intake or audit records. |

### Enforcement boundary

Observe and Advise modes have no write credential to the application control plane. Enforce mode uses a narrowly scoped connector. A failed or compromised monitor cannot obtain unrestricted application credentials.

### Response objective

The goal is a correct, explainable, reversible decision. “Automatic repair” is not an acceptable substitute for evidence, authorization, or outcome verification.

### Real-data policy

Model-quality claims use authorized real data with declared annotation provenance. Constructed attacks remain benchmark evidence, not production-incidence evidence. Synthetic load events can test capacity and faults only. They cannot support a claim about safety accuracy or real-world harm avoided.

### No false proof

A signature proves control of a signing key and integrity of the signed content. It does not prove that a classifier is correct. Hardware attestation, if supported, proves a measured execution context under its threat model. It does not prove that the guardrail blocks every harmful request.

### Time and identity

Use UTC timestamps, source clock information, tenant-scoped actor IDs, and key identifiers. Record clock-skew warnings. Evidence ordering uses durable sequence information, not only wall-clock time.

### Tenant isolation model

Shared deployments use authenticated tenant context at every API, queue, worker, database, object, cache, and export boundary. Dedicated tenants can receive isolated compute, queues, databases, and keys. A dedicated deployment has no undocumented shared-content path.

### Administrative controls

Global support access is off by default. Customer approval, short expiry, and a recorded purpose govern any support session. Administrators cannot grant themselves unrecorded content access or bypass an action approval.

## 13. Statistical assurance and limits

### What the system can conclude

A distribution test can identify changed scores or features under declared assumptions. It does not identify the cause or prove a loss of accuracy. A reference-model disagreement is another signal, not an oracle. Common-mode attacks, shared data, and shared model lineage can defeat both signals.

### Required assessment design

- Reference periods must represent approved traffic and carry version and coverage metadata.
- Monitoring units must account for sessions, bursts, seasonality, and correlated requests.
- Threshold calibration must consider repeated looks, parallel slices, and alert suppression.
- Sequential guarantees apply only under their stated assumptions. Assumption failures must appear in the report.
- The detector must expose minimum count, window duration, cooldown, missing-data behavior, and parameter version.
- Embedding projection is a configurable, evaluated transform. A universal 32-dimension rule is not a product guarantee.

### Alert-quality acceptance

Target: no more than one false page per 168 monitor-hours on the approved nominal-traffic suite. Report observation duration and an uncertainty interval. Correlated streams require block-based analysis. An underpowered test cannot establish the target.

### Change-sensitivity acceptance

For each supported task, define harmful-change and harmless-change scenarios before testing. Target: detect at least 90% of the approved material-change scenarios within two completed assessment windows. Report missed scenarios and false pages. This is a scenario-suite target, not an all-attack guarantee.

### Labelling policy

Use approved random sampling to estimate performance without selection bias. Add an incident-focused sample for investigation, but report it separately. Do not estimate fleet accuracy from escalated cases alone.

### Strongest claim permitted

“Available evidence indicates a change and supports this response.” The product cannot state “all requests are safe” or “no labels are needed for verified repair.”

## 14. Models, calibration, and release policy

### Model registry

Record task, label semantics, architecture, model digest, tokenizer digest, numerical precision, training provenance, license, supported languages, context limit, resource profile, and approved deployment targets. Unsupported inputs receive an explicit coverage state.

### Calibration is not retraining

Calibration changes how a score maps to a decision or uncertainty set. It does not repair the underlying model weights. A candidate calibration requires suitable labels, a justified method, a locked holdout, and a reviewed operating point. Distribution-shift guarantees do not survive arbitrary attack conditions by default.

### Promotion policy

- Evaluate candidate recall, false-alarm rate, uncertainty, and subgroup coverage against the incumbent.
- Default recall non-regression margin: one percentage point. The tenant risk owner can tighten this limit.
- Report confidence intervals and the available sample size. Inconclusive evidence blocks promotion.
- Test context truncation, long requests, language changes, missing scores, and token-budget failure.
- Run in shadow mode before a bounded canary rollout. Hold the rollback version and execution credentials ready.
- Separate the change author from the high-risk approver.

### Automatic action limits

Pre-approved conservative containment can proceed within its contract. A more permissive threshold, model replacement, or new safety taxonomy always requires review. No loop can train on its own predicted labels and then claim independent improvement.

### Change acceptance

A rollback command receives acceptance within 60 seconds at the contracted load. Deployment verification completes within the connector-specific budget. External control-plane delays remain visible and are not reported as a successful repair.

## 15. Event and label data contracts

### Score-event envelope

| Field group | Required semantics |
|---|---|
| Identity | event_id; tenant context from authentication; workspace_id; deployment_id; source_id. |
| Time and order | source_time in UTC; received_time; optional session and sequence IDs; clock-skew status. |
| Versions | schema_version; model_digest; tokenizer_digest where relevant; guardrail_version; policy_version. |
| Task and scores | task_id; category scores; score range; direction; refusal or decision code; truncation and error flags. |
| Observation | sample probability; measured model latency; context length; source and language coverage. |
| Provenance | production or replay; source revision; content-consent class; evidence and feature references. |

### Labels

Labels include event_id, task_id, label definition version, annotator type, source, adjudication state, label_time, and confidence. An unlabelled event remains unlabelled. A model judgement cannot silently become a human label.

### Limits and ordering

- Representative envelope: 2 KiB. Hard score-envelope limit: 8 KiB after decompression.
- Optional feature objects use a separate endpoint with a 128 KiB limit and at most 4,096 numeric dimensions.
- Raw text is disabled by default and follows a separate approved content policy.
- Default live watermark: two minutes. Tenant-approved tolerance cannot exceed ten minutes without a separate capacity review.
- Records up to 24 hours late can enter retrospective assessment, but not a closed live window without a recorded revision.
- Event ID retry window: 72 hours. Action idempotency records follow the longer audit-retention policy.

### Interpretation

A score is not automatically a probability. Score contracts declare its meaning. Cross-model comparisons require a validated mapping, not just a shared 0-to-1 range.

## 16. API and SDK requirements

### External API surface

| Method and path | Purpose | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| POST /v1/events:batch | Submit score events. | Scoped identity, schema validation, idempotency, durable receipt. |
| POST /v1/labels:batch | Submit delayed labels. | Label provenance, task match, adjudication policy. |
| POST /v1/deployments | Register a monitored deployment. | Workspace permission and version contract. |
| POST /v1/baselines | Propose a reference period. | Evidence checks and reviewer approval. |
| GET /v1/monitors/{id} | Read state and coverage. | Tenant scope and explicit stale-data fields. |
| POST /v1/evaluations | Request an isolated replay. | Dataset rights, resource budget, immutable input manifest. |
| POST /v1/incidents/{id}/actions | Propose a response. | Allowlist, approval, version precondition, expiry. |
| POST /v1/actions/{id}/approvals | Approve the exact proposal. | Separate authorized approver and proposal digest. |
| POST /v1/exports | Create an evidence export. | Purpose, content scope, expiry, and audited access. |
| GET /v1/operations/{id} | Read a long-running result. | Bounded status polling and stable operation ID. |

### Contract rules

Publish OpenAPI specifications, typed errors, cursor pagination, rate limits, and retry guidance. HTTP 429 and 503 include bounded retry guidance. Accepted asynchronous jobs return an operation ID. Unknown fields follow a published compatibility policy. Breaking changes require a new major API version and at least 180 days of support for the prior version.

### Batch semantics

A batch contains one tenant and at most 250 events. Its decompressed size cannot exceed 2 MiB. The response identifies accepted, duplicate, and rejected records. Only durably accepted records receive success. Retries use the original event IDs.

### SDKs

GA requires Python, Java 17 or later, and TypeScript clients. Each client supports batching, timeouts, bounded retries, TLS, identity rotation, trace context, and local redaction. Retry behavior cannot duplicate a side effect. The compatibility matrix identifies tested runtime and gateway versions.

Support sessions require customer permission, a purpose, a short expiry, and audit. Break-glass access expires within one hour and requires a post-event review. ## 19. Security requirements and threat model

| ID | Threat or requirement | Required acceptance test |
|---|---|---|
| SEC-01 | Tenant escape through IDs, search, cache, queue, object URLs, or exports. | Cross-tenant tests cover every data path and reject all unauthorized access. |
| SEC-02 | Forged, replayed, or altered telemetry. | Authenticate producers, validate schemas, detect duplicates, and bind event provenance. |
| SEC-03 | Prompt injection against the operator console or analysis pipeline. | Treat all source content as data. No prompt text can create a command, approval, connector, or policy. |
| SEC-04 | Malicious model artifacts and unsafe deserialization. | Require approved digests and formats. No untrusted pickle or remote model code executes in production. |
| SEC-05 | Poisoned labels, reference periods, or calibration sets. | Preserve provenance, separate reviewer roles, detect conflicting labels, and require candidate approval. |
| SEC-06 | Resource exhaustion and alert floods. | Enforce decompressed-size, complexity, time, cardinality, and tenant resource limits. |
| SEC-07 | Stolen enforcement credentials or unsafe outbound connections. | Use scoped secrets, rotation, egress allowlists, SSRF controls, signed webhooks, and replay protection. |
| SEC-08 | Compromised builds or dependencies. | Produce an SBOM, scan dependencies, sign images, verify admission, and publish a vulnerability response process. |
| SEC-09 | Host or administrator access beyond the declared trust boundary. | Publish the deployment threat model. Do not claim protection from a host administrator without qualified confidential-compute controls. |

### Security baseline

Use TLS 1.2 or later for external traffic and approved TLS 1.3 where supported. Encrypt persistent stores with customer-approved keys. Apply least privilege, non-root processes, network policies, and short-lived secrets. No privileged container or Docker socket belongs in the application runtime.

### Release restriction

An independent penetration test, a tenant-isolation review, and a threat-model review are mandatory. No unresolved critical or exploitable high-severity issue can remain at GA. Lower-severity exceptions have owners and expiry dates.

## 20. Privacy, residency, and retention

### Default data posture

Scores and approved metadata are sufficient for the default integration. Prompt and response text stay in the customer application. Content collection requires an explicit purpose, user authority, minimization policy, and retention setting. Low-entropy content hashes can leak information; use tenant-scoped HMAC references where correlation is required.

| ID | Product requirement | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| PRI-01 | Disable raw-content collection by default. | A new tenant emits no prompt or response text into telemetry, logs, alerts, or traces. |
| PRI-02 | Apply field allowlists, redaction, and content classification at the collector. | Disallowed fields cannot cross the tenant's approved data boundary. |
| PRI-03 | Separate encryption keys and access scope by tenant. Support customer-managed keys. | Key rotation and tenant revocation do not expose another tenant's data. |
| PRI-04 | Enforce region constraints for active stores, backups, support, and exports. | A residency test includes notification and recovery paths. |
| PRI-05 | Support purpose-scoped sensitive-data reveal and export. | Every reveal records actor, scope, reason, and expiry. |
| PRI-06 | Process authorized deletion, legal holds, and offboarding with clear exceptions. | A deletion report identifies active-store completion and backup expiry. |
| PRI-07 | Keep content out of product analytics and model training unless separately authorized. | Customer data is not used to improve shared models by default. |
| PRI-08 | Preserve privacy notices, source rights, and customer processing agreements. | Commercial onboarding cannot finish with an unresolved data-rights gate. |

### Proposed default retention

| Data class | Default | Hard control |
|---|---|---|
| Durable queue | 24 hours. | Quota, encryption, and lag alarms. |
| Searchable event metadata | 7 days. | Partition expiry; customer-configurable reduction. |
| Archived score envelopes | 30 days. | Tenant key and object lifecycle. |
| Raw text | Off; at most 7 days when enabled. | Separate approval and access policy. |
| Incident and configuration audit | 365 days. | No raw text; declared legal-hold exception. |
| Backups | 35 days. | Restore reapplies deletion tombstones before access. |

Authorized deletion targets active stores within 24 hours. Backups expire within 35 days unless a legal hold applies. Product and Legal resolve retention conflicts before a tenant receives a contract.

## 21. Evidence, audit, and governance outputs

### Evidence package

Use per-tenant append-only audit sequences and signed checkpoints. Anchor checkpoints outside the mutable application store. A deletion tombstone records what is no longer available without retaining the erased content. Retained checkpoint integrity and privacy rights require a reviewed key and retention design.

SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are assurance-program objectives only. No certification claim is allowed before independent completion. Any FIPS claim requires a qualified cryptographic module and approved configuration. Deployment on IBM Z alone does not establish that claim.

## 22. Reference product architecture

[ARCHITECTURE]

### System boundaries

The **data plane** receives tenant telemetry, maintains assessment state, and stores approved event evidence inside the customer-approved region. The **control plane** holds configuration, roles, policies, approvals, and deployment metadata. A separate **enforcement boundary** owns the authority to change an application.

### Selected logical services

- Collectors authenticate and minimize data before intake.
- A replicated Apache Kafka event bus provides durable, partitioned delivery.
- Stateless intake workers validate envelopes and apply quotas.
- Stateful assessment workers use versioned windows and checkpoints.
- An incident service joins qualified signals with labels and deployment changes.
- An evaluation service runs isolated replay jobs without production action credentials.
- A policy and approval service authorizes bounded actions.
- PostgreSQL stores control records and indexed operational metadata.
- An S3-compatible object store retains scoped evidence and score archives.
- A constrained action service connects to the customer's enforcement point.

### Architectural acceptance

The design must prove tenant isolation, durable acknowledgement, controlled cardinality, backpressure, independent recovery, and auditable action. Component selection alone does not establish those properties. Broker, database, object-store, and orchestration distributions require deployment-specific qualification.

## 24. Scale and capacity contract

### Reference workload W1 · proposed qualification target

| Dimension | Target or assumption |
|---|---|
| Regional intake | 1,000 score events per second sustained for 24 hours. |
| Burst | 5,000 events per second for five minutes with bounded recovery. |
| Tenancy | 100 active tenants and 5,000 registered deployment streams per region. |
| Event size | 2 KiB average; 8 KiB score-envelope limit. |
| Queries | 50 concurrent console users with bounded tenant and time filters. |
| Reference inference | Independently metered and capped. Not included as 1,000 model inferences per second. |
| Placement | At least three worker replicas across declared independent failure domains. |
| Capacity reserve | At least 30% measured headroom at W1 after one planned worker drain. |

### Storage arithmetic

At 1,000 events per second, W1 produces 86.4 million events per day. At 2 KiB per event, this is about **176.95 GB per day** before compression, indexes, or replication. Thirty days require about **5.31 TB** for one archive copy. A replicated queue and hot indexes add separate capacity. Compression and deduplication savings cannot enter a quote without measurement.

### Scale controls

Partition by tenant and stream identity. Keep per-stream order where available. Isolate replay, query, and reference-inference pools from live assessment. Apply tenant quotas before expensive work. Autoscaling uses intake, lag, window-state size, and resource limits. Tenant sharding and regional placement prevent a single unbounded shared database from becoming the scaling plan.

### Qualification rule

W1 is a target, not a benchmark result. The present 3-vCPU VM cannot establish enterprise capacity. Production sizing requires measured service curves, failure tests, storage growth, and model-specific inference costs. Define larger workload profiles before promising more throughput.

## 25. Service objectives and performance

All values below are proposed GA acceptance targets under W1. They exclude invalid or unauthorized requests, but include server failures and planned service maintenance. Customer-originated network faults are reported separately.

| ID | Objective | Measurement and acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| NFR-01 | Intake API availability: 99.95% per calendar month. | Valid authorized submissions with successful durable receipt divided by valid authorized attempts. Publish the denominator. |
| NFR-02 | Console and control API availability: 99.9% per month. | External probes plus real requests; no hidden planned-maintenance exclusion. |
| NFR-03 | Intake latency: p95 <=100 ms; p99 <=250 ms. | Server-side receipt to durable acknowledgement at W1. Model inference is a separate budget. |
| NFR-04 | Assessment lag: p95 <=10 seconds; p99 <=30 seconds. | Durable receipt to window-state update after required samples are available. Not a guaranteed time from attack onset. |
| NFR-05 | Notification dispatch: p95 <=30 seconds. | Incident creation to accepted first delivery attempt. Third-party receipt remains separately visible. |
| NFR-06 | Standard console query: p95 <=2 seconds. | First page of at most 100 rows within one tenant and a one-hour filter. Large exports are asynchronous. |

### Error-budget policy

A 99.95% objective permits 21.6 minutes of unavailability in a 30-day month. A 99.9% objective permits 43.2 minutes. Exhaustion pauses nonessential production changes. SRE and Product approve recovery work before normal release cadence resumes.

### Detection time is evidence-dependent

End-to-end signal time includes minimum sample collection, window completion, and processing lag. Confirmed performance loss also includes label delay. Low traffic cannot support a seconds-scale accuracy guarantee.

## 26. High availability and disaster recovery

### Failure model

Design for worker death, broker loss, database failover, object-store errors, network partitions, regional loss, identity outages, and customer enforcement outages. High availability requires independent failure domains. Multiple containers on one VM do not satisfy this requirement.

| Failure | Required behavior | Recovery proof |
|---|---|---|
| Worker restart | Resume versioned state from the last durable checkpoint. | No duplicate action and no false “healthy” reset. |
| One broker or failure domain | Preserve durable receipt within the qualified quorum policy. | Acknowledged events remain recoverable. Intake backpressures when quorum is lost. |
| Metadata primary failure | Fence the old writer and fail over without approval-history loss. | Read and write invariants pass after promotion. |
| Archive outage | Buffer within budget and display evidence lag. | No evidence-complete claim before archive confirmation. |
| Control-plane outage | Continue approved observation where cached policy permits. Freeze unapproved changes. | Existing policy and fallback remain explicit. |
| Region loss | Restore in an approved recovery location. | Residency, keys, tombstones, and approval state survive the drill. |

### Recovery objectives

Target regional RTO: 60 minutes. Target regional RPO: at most 15 minutes for telemetry and at most five minutes for control metadata. Within one failure domain, acknowledged-event durability has a zero-loss target. Regional disaster loss limits remain explicit and do not inherit that zero-loss claim.

### Backup and rehearsal

Encrypt backups with independent access controls. Verify restores quarterly and after major storage changes. Reconcile sequence gaps before resuming alerts. Apply deletion tombstones before restored data becomes accessible. Rehearse key loss, secret rotation, and stale approvals. Publish a drill report with measured recovery times and residual gaps.

## 27. Operability and monitor health

### Monitor the monitor

A separate health path measures collector freshness, intake success, queue lag, worker progress, checkpoint age, label backlog, alert delivery, action reconciliation, and evidence-store lag. This path uses its own alerts and cannot depend only on the component under test.

### Required operating signals

- Rate, errors, latency, and saturation by service and bounded tenant class.
- Active and missing streams; schema errors; late-data counts; deduplication counts.
- Completed windows; evidence counts; reference age; label coverage; detector failures.
- Open incidents; notification failures; approval age; expired actions; rollback results.
- Storage growth; key expiry; signing failures; backup and restore freshness.

### Alert policy

Separate service incidents from content-risk incidents. Deduplicate, route, and suppress by approved maintenance policies. Preserve suppressed counts. Use severity, owner, and runbook links. A page contains evidence and a clear next action, not raw customer text.

### Operational practices

Use versioned runbooks, staged releases, feature flags, dependency inventories, capacity reviews, and incident postmortems. Apply OpenTelemetry-compatible traces and metrics through a pinned semantic-convention mapping. Do not assume evolving GenAI attributes remain stable across upgrades.

### On-call acceptance

SRE can diagnose an intake outage, a stuck window, an expired key, a failed export, and an enforcement failure from documented telemetry. A simulated total monitor outage must produce an independent service alarm within two minutes.

### Terms with fixed meaning

**Guardrail:** a safety control that evaluates or constrains AI traffic. It is not necessarily a single small classifier.

**Drift:** a change in an observed distribution or relationship. Drift does not always reduce accuracy.

**Reference model:** an optional comparison signal. It is not a trusted source of ground truth.

**Calibration:** the relationship between model scores, decisions, uncertainty, and observed labels. It does not repair weights.

**Coverage:** the declared proportion of traffic, labels, slices, or prediction sets represented by evidence. The UI states which definition applies.

**Attestation:** a verifiable statement about a measured execution context under a specific hardware trust model.

**Evidence complete:** all items required by the declared evidence policy are present. It does not mean that the AI system is safe.

### Claims the product must not make

- “Nobody monitors guardrails” or “no competing product exists.” The competitive study remains open.
- “New publication means the data is absent from model training.” No such training-set audit exists.
- “No labels are needed to prove performance or repair calibration.” Labels remain necessary for those claims.
- “Different models cannot fail together.” Common-mode failure remains possible.
- “A hash chain is hardware attestation” or “hardware attestation proves safety.” Neither statement is correct.
- “This PRD makes the current server production-ready.” Deployment and release gates remain unmet.
